## Question

"What happens if a Consumer Group has fewer consumer instances than the topic has Partitions?"

## What the interviewer wants to assess

The counterpoint to the previous question: tests whether the candidate understands the opposite scenario
and its load-balancing implications, not just the "ideal" 1:1 case.

<RespostaCurta>
Some consumers receive more than one partition. Processing stays parallel (every partition still has
exactly one consumer assigned), but becomes unbalanced, since consumers with multiple partitions process
more load than the others.
</RespostaCurta>

<RespostaSenior>
Kafka distributes the remaining partitions among the available consumers as evenly as possible (depending
on the configured assignor strategy), but if the number of partitions isn't a multiple of the number of
consumers, the distribution is never perfectly equal: one consumer might get 3 partitions and another 2,
for example. That's not a problem by itself, but it means the consumer with more partitions assigned tends
to accumulate more consumer lag (Chapter 15) if volume per partition is similar across them, because it
has to process twice or three times as many messages in the same time window.
</RespostaSenior>

## In-depth explanation

See "What happens with different numbers of consumers" in
[Chapter 6](/en/kafka/livro/consumer-groups-e-rebalance).

<ExemploFinanceiro>
A topic with 5 partitions and a Consumer Group with 2 replicas results in one replica processing 3
partitions and the other processing 2, still parallel, but with one replica absorbing 50% more load than
the other.
</ExemploFinanceiro>

<Pegadinha title='"This is a problem I need to fix immediately"'>
Not always. It's a valid, functional distribution; the real warning sign is when that imbalance turns
into disproportionate, sustained consumer lag on one of the instances, a sign that the group's total
parallelism is insufficient for the volume, not just poorly distributed.
</Pegadinha>
